I've always been the type to mess with things just to see how they work.


Somewhere between coding, games, editing, AI tools, and building random ideas that probably didn't need to exist, I ended up becoming interested in making things myself. I like the process of taking something apart, figuring it out, changing it, and eventually turning it into something that actually feels like mine.


But coding is only one side of it. I've always had a pretty strong visual and artistic side too. I like playing around with composition, typography, colour, imagery, music, motion and atmosphere. Whether I'm editing an image, designing something, working on a game, or putting together an interface, I tend to obsess over the little details that most people probably wouldn't notice. Sometimes I'll spend more time getting something to feel right than I spent making it work in the first place.


That's probably why I enjoy projects where the technical and creative sides overlap. I don't just want something to function — I want it to have an identity. A good interface, animation, piece of artwork or interactive experience can communicate something without having to explain itself.


I'm currently studying Computer Science with AI / ML, but I don't want my entire identity to be a list of technologies. I'm interested in pretty much anything where code, creativity and experimentation overlap — from software and AI to interactive experiences, visual design, games, editing and whatever else I happen to get obsessed with next.


A lot of what I know has come from simply wanting to make something and figuring out how to get there. I'll jump between completely different things, learn what I need, break something, fix it, redesign it, and probably change it again because I decided I didn't like how it looked.


I'm still figuring out exactly where all of this takes me. For now, I'm just making things, learning as I go, and seeing what I can make.
